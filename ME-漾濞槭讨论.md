In the present study, we sampled 105 individuals of the 703 known extant *Acer yangbiense* plants, and addressed conservation issues in *A*. *yangbiense* using whole genome resequencing data. These data suggest factors that may have contributed to the formation of the small, isolated population sizes and low genetic diversity currently seen in this species, as well as which threats it is currently confronted with. It is our hope that following the interpretation of these results and through cooperation with relevant stakeholders, it will be possible to implement meaningful conservation actions.

*Acer yangbiense* currently maintains a lower genetic diversity compared with other studied trees (Figure [S12](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#support-information-section); Table [S20](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#support-information-section)), owing to at least three factors. (i) Bottleneck events, particularly the most recent, decreased *Ne* to <200, and brought *A*. *yangbiense* to the edge of extinction. The effects of this are still being felt, despite the subsequent partial recovery of *Ne*. Particularly for *A*. *yangbiense*, a long-lived woody plant (referring to the overall time of species fertility) with low ability to regenerate, we would not expect accumulations of many mutations that could increase genetic diversity over short periods of thousands of years. (ii) The estimated contemporary *Ne* is 23.9, and the very low ratio (~0.03) of contemporary *Ne* to the census population size (703) suggests that *A*. *yangbiense* has suffered a recent decline in population size (Turner et al., [2006](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-bib-0046)). Habitat fragmentation and human disturbance further accelerated the effect of genetic drift and further reduced genetic diversity. Intensifying anthropogenic activities, including agricultural activities, logging, irrigation construction, vegetation clearance and deforestation, burning and grazing, all probably contributed to the fragmentation of the *A*. *yangbiense* habitat (Tao et al., [2020](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-bib-0044)). During field investigations, we observed that several populations of *A*. *yangbiense* were surrounded by farmland and roads, which made its regeneration very difficult. Moreover, of the 10 known populations, only four comprised more than 100 individuals (Tao et al., [2020](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-bib-0044)). (iii) A biased sex ratio in mature *A*. *yangbiense* individuals does not allow for sufficient outcrossing, preventing production of outcrossed seeds, which would be an important way for increasing the genetic diversity of the populations. Field investigations by Tao et al. ([2020](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-bib-0044)) suggested that only 5.37% of trees could produce mature fruits, which is exacerbated by the dominance of male-flowered individuals.

We employed three methods of analysis to infer the demographic history of *A*. *yangbiense*, which depicted changes of *Ne* and population divergences over time. Several ancient events might have been responsible for its current demography. As shown in the result of fastsimcoal2 (Figure [3e](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-fig-0003)), CR and OTHERS diverged at ~52 ka, coinciding with the MIS 3b stage (Marine Isotope Stage 3b, 54–44 ka) of the Dali Glaciation, a significantly cold stage (Cui et al., [2011](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-bib-0007)). In addition, the first bottleneck uncovered in fastsimcoal2 coincided with the “Middle Pleistocene Revolution” ~1.2–0.7 Ma, a widespread event characterized by severe and prolonged cold stages, resulting in an increase in Asian aridity and monsoonal intensity (Clark et al., [2006](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-bib-0006); Head & Gibbard, [2005](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-bib-0018)). This event was also identified using stairway plot 2 (Figure [3a,b](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-fig-0003)).

From ~8 ka to ~1500 years ago, *A*. *yangbiense* also suffered from severe population contraction (Figure [3a–d](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-fig-0003)), which might relate to anthropogenic activities. Pollen records suggest that anthropogenic disturbance occurred ~6370 years ago in the Cangshan Mountains, when forests were being cleared for shifting agriculture (Shen et al., [2006](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-bib-0041)). Similarly, evidence of significant later anthropogenic disturbance has been found in the area, including during the Neolithic (~3100 years ago) and Bronze Age (>2000 years ago) (Wan et al., [2011](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-bib-0047)), and evidence of inward migration and establishment of the Han Chinese (~2300–1700 years ago) (Dearing et al., [2008](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-bib-0009)). All these events probably shaped the demography of *A*. *yangbiense*, in particular the sharp decline in *Ne* in the most recent bottleneck, and are probably responsible for the small and fragmented populations of this species. It should be noted that the inconsistent results resulting from the different methods of analysis are to be expected. This is due to the fact that smc++ is a method that uses the sequentially Markovian coalescent to account for structural/linkage information, as well as the SFS in its estimates of historical demography. The stairway plot 2 and fastsimcoal2 methods are based on SFS, and the latter two methods do not take into account linkage or contiguity among sites, which might yield different estimated population size changes and different estimated timings (Patton et al., [2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-bib-0035)). Furthermore, parameter estimates of fastsimcoal2 mainly depend on which types of demographic scenarios are assumed (and these are sometimes defined in a somewhat arbitrary manner), which could also yield inconsistent results when compared with other methods (Excofffier et al., [2021](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-bib-0013)). Another caveat is that small changes in estimated generation time could impact the inference of demographic time. However, we have attempted to assign a reasonable estimate of generation time, which was calculated from the personal observations that it takes between 8 and 10 years for *A*. *yangbiense* seed planted in the field and in gardens to germinate and begin to flower.

As well as revealing factors that have potentially decreased the genetic variation in *A*. *yangbiense* as discussed above, our conservation genomic analysis also suggested the clear presence of severe genetic threats in the small isolated populations of this species. The MLT and LSBD populations had significantly higher FROH than did the CR, PJH, DYD and BD populations (Figure [4a](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-fig-0004)). Inbreeding in MLT was not unexpected, as only five individuals are present in the wild in this population. Furthermore, the possibility that the MLT population was originally established by a single or only few fruits with the same genetic background could not be ruled out. Although the LSBD population comprises 77 individuals, only one single individual set fruits when we performed field investigations in 2016. Additionally, LSBD also had the largest numbers of homozygous deleterious variants (Figure [4b,c](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-fig-0004)), most of which were unique to this population. This phenomenon could be explained by (i) slower removal of homozygous (particularly deleterious) variants due to the limited chance of producing sufficient seedlings for natural selection (e.g., purging selection for severely deleterious homozygous variants) (Glémin, [2003](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-bib-0017)); and (ii) failure to acquire sufficient gene flow from other populations. Indeed, this is supported by the results from our treemix analysis, which suggested that gene flow had only occurred in the distant past (e.g., between ancestral populations of DYD and DYS), and no gene flow was detected between LSBD and any other current population. Conversely, although it received no gene flow from other populations (Figure [2c](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-fig-0002)), the CR population had the smallest level of inbreeding, as indicated by its lowest FROH (Figure [4a](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-fig-0004)), which might be attributable to its relatively larger census population size (126 individuals), leading to increased opportunity for outcrossing. Overall, differing opportunities for recombination, determined probably by the chance of cross-pollination and different amount of gene flow due to habitat fragmentation, contributed to the observed differences in patterns of genetic load among populations of *A*. *yangbiense*.

GO annotation of these deleterious mutations indicated that most were enriched in particular for annotated functions involved in defence (Table [S19](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#support-information-section)). For example, the flavonoid biosynthetic process (GO: 0009813), which plays a major role in plant defence response, was determined as one of the top enriched GO terms. This is involved in biosynthetic and metabolic processes concerning secondary metabolites such as hydroxycinnamic acids, stilbenes, lignans and lignins (Ogo et al., [2015](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-bib-0034); Quideau, [2006](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-bib-0038)). Moreover, flavonoids are important in protecting plants from the potential hazards of ultraviolet (UV) radiation (Koes et al., [1994](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-bib-0021)). Some of the other genes among the top annotated GO terms (*p* < .05) were also important in plant defence responses, for example *Acyan01G0206700* is included in “defense response” (GO:0006952) and “terpenoid biosynthetic” (GO:0016114) activities. Similarly, the GO annotation included the genes associated with “response to symbiotic fungus,” “defense response to insect” and “response to toxic substances.” All of these genes are associated with deleterious mutations which may lower the ability of *A*. *yangbiense* to adapt to harmful environments, and might also explain why nearly all seedlings and saplings, and all adult individuals of *A*. *yangbiense*, were found to be vulnerable to fungal infection (Figure [S13](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#support-information-section)). Consistent with this, a recent finding revealed that *A*. *yangbiense* had a very low number of structure-intact Nucleotide-binding site (NBS)-encoding genes, which have been demonstrated to play a role in immune signalling, when compared to two other species in the family Sapindaceae (Zhou et al., [2020](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-bib-0056)). Furthermore, deleterious mutations related to “recognition of pollen” (GO: 0048544), “rejection of self pollen” (GO: 0060320), “floral organ development” (GO:0048437) and “pollen sperm cell differentiation” (GO: 0048235) were also enriched, which may provide an explanation for the biased gender ratio and different flower types in *A*. *yangbiense*. However, these gene ontology results need further investigation before firm conclusions can be drawn.

Our comprehensive conservation genomic results provide solid implications for further *A*. *yangbiense* conservation action. (i) Evidently the populations MX and MLT had the lowest nucleotide diversity (Table [1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-tbl-0001)), with comparatively higher FROH and more homozygous DEL variants (Figure [4a](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-fig-0004)). DHY seemed to be the ideal source population for MX and MLT, due to its close geographical distance (Figure [1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-fig-0001)) and similar genetic background (forming linkage 6 with MLT; MX was the population that was least genetically different from DYD). We suggest that seedlings germinated from seeds produced by hand-cross pollinations in DHY are introduced into the MLT and MX populations, increasing the genetic diversity for both of these populations. (ii) LSBD also needs further conservation action due to its high FROH and large number of homozygous DEL variants, of which 786 were private. Genetic rescue might be an option in this case, and we suggest that female flowers in LSBD are pollinated with pollen grains collected from the DYS population. We suggest this for two reasons. First, DYS had the lowest number of homozygous DEL variants (145, Figure [4c](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mec.16289?saml_referrer#mec16289-fig-0004)), and therefore has the highest chance to make most of the homozygous DEL variants heterozygous, but without bringing in further homozygous DEL variants to LSBD. Second, the populations have similar genetic background and a low *F*ST (0.137) between them. CR might be also a candidate source population due to its distinct and pure genetic background, low FROH and low numbers of homozygous DEL variants. However, caution should be applied and the possibility of outbreeding depression should be evaluated before reintroduction programmes are implemented, due to the higher *F*ST of CR than all other populations. Future research towards sex variation in *A*. *yangbiense* is encouraged, in order to reduce the bias between male and female trees in the wild and ultimately to strengthen the normal regeneration of *A*. *yangbiense* in its natural habitat.
